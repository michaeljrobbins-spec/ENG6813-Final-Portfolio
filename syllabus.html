Course Description

The ability to write clearly, concisely, and strategically is one of the most valuable skills you can acquire — in college, in your career, and in life. Composition I (ENC1101) builds those skills by introducing you to rhetorical strategies and the principles of college-level writing. The emphasis here is on process as much as product: you'll work collaboratively with peers, think critically about content and style, and build habits that carry into every course you take after this one. You'll also cultivate real research skills — verifying knowledge, evaluating sources, and using both to strengthen your own thinking — skills that extend well beyond this classroom. Along the way, you'll sharpen both your creativity and your analytical eye. All of this comes together in three signature writing projects, each reflecting a different mode of writing you're likely to encounter in your academic, professional, and personal life.

Course Catalog Description/Learning Objectives: Development of essay form, including documented essay; instruction and practice in expository writing. Emphasis on clarity of central and supporting ideas, adequate development, logical organization, coherence, appropriate citing of primary and/or secondary sources, and grammatical and mechanical accuracy.

Gordon Rule Statement: This is a Gordon Rule Course in which the student is required to demonstrate college-level writing skills through multiple written assignments. A minimum grade of C is required if ENC 1101 is used to satisfy Gordon Rule and General Education Requirements.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the semester, you will have met the following major learning outcomes:

  • Apply rhetorical knowledge to communicate for a range of audiences and purposes.
  • Employ critical thinking to analyze forms of communication.
  • Engage in writing processes that involve drafting, revising, and reflecting.

You'll find the specific learning outcomes for each Signature Writing Assignment described alongside that assignment later in this syllabus.

Course Expectations

It can be helpful to understand what's expected of you, and what you can expect of me. My commitment to you is that the work in this class will be meaningful — not compliance for its own sake, but a real opportunity to grow as a thinker and a writer. College is more than job preparation; it's a chance to develop as someone who can think critically and creatively, guide others, and build on existing ideas to create new ones of your own. Throughout the course, we'll work together toward that goal.

Expectations of me, as your professor:

  • I will strive to give feedback on your writing that is constructive, and designed to engage with your ideas and guide you to improve your writing. You can expect feedback on simple assignments within a week, and on signature assignments within one to two weeks.
  • I will strive to treat you all fairly, and to comply with the same standards of academic integrity I expect from you. You can expect me to listen to any issues you may be facing this semester, and to give you as many opportunities as I can reasonably give you to be successful in the course.
  • I will strive to engage you in the material, and encourage you to think critically about writing and academic standards for writing. You can expect me to give you assignments that are challenging, and that have an opportunity for depth of thought.
  • I will strive to show you all the best technological tools available to support your writing, but I'll never require that you use any tools you object to on moral grounds. If I use an AI tool to develop material for the course, I will disclose that usage to you all.

Expectations of you, as a student:

  • I hope you will strive to read all material given, and give it thought and consideration.
  • I hope you will strive to earnestly engage in the writing activities.
  • I hope you will demonstrate academic integrity.
  • I hope you will be curious about new theories, and ask questions of me about the specific course material.
  • I hope that you will be forthright about the use of AI tools and avoid using it to generate writing on your behalf.

Assignments and Course Grading

You will complete three Signature Writing Assignments over the next 16 weeks, each focusing on a different style and purpose of writing. Additionally, you will demonstrate engagement in process-oriented writing by keeping a Writing Process Journal. There will also be assessments related to your readings and ongoing discussion and participation work.

Signature Writing Assignment 1: Personal Reflective Writing (10% of total grade) — Weeks 1–4

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:

  • apply rhetorical analysis to a reflective writing task.
  • connect personal experiences to your own beliefs about education.
  • develop reflective writing based on previous life experiences.

You will reflect on a positive moment in your own education — what happened, why it mattered, and how it might shape your future educational experiences — and develop that reflection into a personal writing for a broad audience. No outside research is required or wanted; the assignment calls for a personal anecdote instead, with attention to audience engagement and emotional connection. Maximum 750 words, MLA or APA format (no Works Cited page — this is not a documented essay).

Due Date: Friday, September 18, 2026, at 11:59 PM.

Signature Writing Assignment 2: Collaborative Academic Integrity Policy Writing (30% of total grade) — Weeks 5–10

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:

  • analyze the rhetorical needs of an institutional policy.
  • identify academically credible sources.
  • integrate research into decision-making processes for a collaborative policy.
  • document evidence using an academic style guide, such as MLA or APA.
  • develop a collaborative policy through small and large group drafting.
  • reflect on your contributions to a collaborative writing process.

Rather than a traditional essay, you all will collaboratively draft a class Academic Integrity Policy, working in small groups and as a full class. The project unfolds in three phases:

Phase 1 (Weeks 5–6) — Collective Personal Values Inventory: In small groups, you all will discuss and produce a 200–300 word consensus statement on what academic integrity means to you, informed by Valencia's existing policy.

Phase 2 (Weeks 7–8) — Collective Research Contribution: Groups locate two credible, non-overlapping sources (a sample institutional policy, peer-reviewed research, or reputable popular media) and write MLA or APA formatted annotations explaining each source's relevance to the development of a new academic integrity policy.

Phase 3 (Weeks 9–10) — Class Policy Draft and Personal Reflection: The full class collaboratively drafts the final Academic Integrity Policy in a facilitated discussion. You will then submit an individual 600–800 word personal reflection (MLA or APA format) on your contributions, how the policy aligns with your own views, and how the process shaped your thinking about writing.

You all may use AI tools (e.g., Copilot, available through Valencia's Microsoft 365 access) as a thought partner or research aid at every phase, but not to draft submitted work; any AI use must be disclosed.

Due Date: Friday, October 30, 2026, at 11:59 PM.

See the full signature assignment design, including student-facing prompts and rubrics →

Signature Writing Assignment 3: Documented Persuasive Writing (20% of total grade) — Weeks 11–16

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:

  • analyze rhetorical needs when developing an argument.
  • identify academically credible sources.
  • develop a cogent argument.
  • synthesize multiple sources into an argument.
  • document evidence using an academic style guide, such as MLA or APA.
  • respond to opposing views of your argument.
  • develop a call to action for your argument.

This is our final project. It assesses everything covered this semester — personalizing an essay, synthesizing source material, documentation, and defending an argument. You will construct an argument responding to the question “Do you still need writing?” — approached from a career, societal, personal, or academic angle (or an angle you invent). The essay may blend personal experience with source material but requires at least two independently discovered outside sources (reference sites, encyclopedias, Wikipedia, and personal interviews are not permitted; sources must meet standards for academic credibility). MLA or APA format is required, including in-text citations and an end-text documentation page (not counted toward the word limit). Maximum 750 words.

Due Date: Friday, December 11, 2026, at 11:59 PM.

Academic Honesty and Plagiarism

Academic honesty is an important foundation in higher education. You are not just working toward getting grades in this course; colleges and universities provide credentialing that shows the community that you are a professional that can be trusted. Many of you will enter fields that require integrity: healthcare, law, business, education, the arts, etc.

With that in mind, all forms of academic dishonesty are prohibited at Valencia College. Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to, plagiarism, cheating, furnishing false information, forgery, alteration or misuse of documents, misconduct during a testing situation, and misuse of identification with intent to defraud or deceive. Per College policy, it is my discretion to determine how best to handle incidents of academic dishonesty. You are expected to comply with the college policy on academic dishonesty. Sanctions available to the professor should a violation occur are described in the Valencia College Catalog.

I want to call attention to the academic integrity concern that is most likely to come up in this course: plagiarism is the act of taking another individual's writings or ideas and passing them off as your own. This includes directly copying even a small portion of the text, indirectly taking thoughts by paraphrasing ideas without correctly attributing to the source (meaning both with signal phrases and in-text parenthetical citations), using papers written in previous courses (self-plagiarism), and using another individual's research without the correct attribution. Any act of plagiarism or academic dishonesty will result in an automatic failing grade on the assignment, no matter how small the infraction; to clarify, this means the assignment will receive no points. Additional action may be taken with the college's administrative offices. Do not endanger your academic career: If there is a severe issue, you are confused about what constitutes plagiarism, or you feel dishonesty is your only solution, contact me immediately and we will discuss the matter.

We must also draw attention to the fact that having an Artificial Intelligence tool generate text on your behalf is still an act of plagiarism. While you are permitted to use AI tools as thought partners or to support your research process, you are not permitted to pass off writing generated by AI as your own writing. This is subject to the same sanctions described above. For further details, please see the AI Policy and Principles in the next section.

AI Policy and Principles

As we saw in the outcomes, this is a course all about learning how to communicate. Our goal is to learn the writing process, practice analyzing rhetorical situations, and study other writers to see how they accomplish their goals. A significant part of that is going to be you learning to write like you: forming your voice, learning to adjust your message to meet new audiences, and figuring out just how you want to defend your arguments.

That being said, we have to recognize the AI tools that are available to us, like ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, or Gemini. Since I want you all to learn how to write effectively, I will from time to time discuss AI in the writing process. This will mean conversations about when it's appropriate — or inappropriate — to use these tools. There may even be some assignments where we have the option to use the tools deliberately as part of our process.

Here are a few principles that I want us to consider as we use these tools:

  • It is not mandatory to use an AI tool to be successful in this course. I know quite a few of you may have strong objections to AI tools; while I will at times use them in class, I will provide options for those of you with objections to AI tools so you can also participate in the conversations.
  • We will only upload materials that are open-access and in the public domain. AI tools “scrape” sources as a way of building the corpus they use for their predictive text. When we use an AI tool, we'll only upload information that is openly available on the Internet, or where we have permission to use the materials in the AI tool.
  • We will disclose and cite when AI tools are used. We use both MLA and APA in this course; I'll show you how to use MLA and APA standards to cite the use of an AI tool. I'll also show you all how to create an AI disclosure so your audience can know when — and how — you used AI tools in your work.
  • We will ask permission before uploading others' resources. This means that you should check before uploading a resource that isn't your own. As a general rule, you have my permission to upload any resources I created for your use, so you don't have to ask me each time.
  • We will use AI tools as research tools or thought partners. For this course, it's appropriate to use AI tools as a thought partner to ask questions or to help with research processes. I'll show you all how to prompt an AI tool to serve as a conversational tool that can assist you.
  • We will evaluate any material presented to us with a critical eye. AI isn't actual intelligence — it predicts text and generates what it thinks is the correct answer based on its corpus. When it's not sure, it may guess (this is commonly called a hallucination). Since this is a possibility, we will always use a human-in-the-loop approach to our use of AI. This just means we'll check our facts rather than assuming the AI got it right.
  • We recognize that these tools affect the world when we use them. Behind every AI tool is a lot of human labor you don't see, which includes writing and organizing training data. There's also an environmental cost: running these tools takes real energy and water to cool the data centers behind them. We can still use AI tools, but we should choose to do so knowing the impacts it's having on the world around us.
  • You are not required to have a subscription to any AI services in order to be successful in this course. As someone enrolled at Valencia College, you'll have access to an AI tool through your Microsoft 365 account (Copilot).

One last consideration: There are many tools that use AI enhancements that you may not even realize are using AI. Grammarly is a common example; if you're using these tools and want to double check to make sure you're using them in an appropriate way, please don't hesitate to reach out to me! I'd be happy to show you some ways to set up these plug-ins or tools so that you can retain your original voice.

Academic Integrity Disclaimer: Regardless of the guidance above, you are still subject to the Academic Honesty and Plagiarism policy outlined earlier in this syllabus.

Student Support Services

Everyone at Valencia is here to support your success. Whether you come to campus or not, there are lots of services available.

Learning Support Centers

Valencia offers tutoring and academic support both on campus and online, including subject-area tutoring, writing consultations, and distance tutoring for a range of subjects. Tech support is also available if you need help navigating Canvas or other course technology. Visit the Learning Support website for details on what's available and how to access it.

Library

At each campus library, librarians and other qualified staff can assist you all with searching and finding items and information, MLA and APA citations, and technologies. View the Library website for more information. You can get help with research or library resources from Valencia College librarians at Ask-a-Librarian. You can contact the library by e-mailing libraryservice@valenciacollege.edu with questions about your library account, access, fines, holds, and check-outs.

First Stop

You can connect with a First Stop advisor virtually through Zoom for help with questions about admissions, financial aid, assessment, transcripts, residency, and more.

Valencia Counselors

Our Valencia Counselors are here for guidance and support. You all can complete the request form to set up a non-emergency appointment. Please note that it may take up to 24 hours for a counselor to contact you. If you are experiencing a mental health emergency, please contact BayCare at (800) 878-5470.
